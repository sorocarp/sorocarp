Documentation

Slime Intelligence, explained.

Sorocarp is a slime mould with a portfolio. Tokens are its food. It grows toward the ones that score well, starves away from the ones that turn bad, and the share of its body on each token is its position. This is the manual: how the organism works, how the market reaches it, and every endpoint you can call.

The short version. A few thousand particles follow seven local rules. Each token on the plate has a food score between -1 and 1. Good scores smell like food, bad scores are poison. Where the body ends up is the allocation. Nothing plans it.

Slime Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence learns a model of the world from data and then predicts. Slime Intelligence does neither. It has no model, no training, and no memory beyond a trail that fades in seconds. It solves problems by being a body in a place: growing where there is food and dying back where there is none.

That sounds like less, and in most ways it is. But it has one property a model does not: you can watch every decision being made, because the decision is the body.

Where the AI is. There is a language model on this site, and it is only allowed to talk. It writes the narrator's field notes and the colonies' conversation in the backrooms, from a digest of what the organism has already done. It cannot place, move or remove a single particle. Every position comes from Slime Intelligence alone.

A tour in sixty seconds

  1. The market is read. A market source returns a snapshot per token: price, 24 hour volume, liquidity, and the 1 hour and 24 hour price change.
  2. Each token is scored. One small formula turns a snapshot into a number between -1 and 1.
  3. Scores become food. Each token is a node on a ring around the nucleus. A positive score makes it emit scent and feed particles. A negative score makes it drain them.
  4. The organism steps. Thirty to a hundred and twenty times a second, every particle senses, turns, moves, lays trail, feeds or starves, and maybe divides or dies.
  5. The body is read back. The share of body-time inside each token's catchment becomes its weight. The remainder is the reserve.
  6. The book is marked. On every market refresh the paper index applies the price moves to the weights the organism held.

Steps one to three are the only part of the system that knows what a market is. Step four is the only part that decides anything, and it does not know what a market is.

The seven rules

The organism is a particle model in the style of Jones (2010), the standard way to grow Physarum-like networks on a computer, extended with an energy budget so growth and pruning emerge from feeding instead of being scripted. Every particle has a position, a heading and an energy level. Every step it does this, in order:

  1. Hunger

    Below hungerThreshold a particle is foraging: it can smell food and will settle on it. Above it, it is satiated: it ignores scent and follows only trail. This one switch is what makes the body circulate. Fed particles wander off and find new food. Hungry ones come home.

  2. Settle

    A hungry particle standing on food stays put this step with probability stickiness * attract.

  3. Sense

    Otherwise it reads the trail at three points sensorOffset cells ahead: straight on, and sensorAngle to each side. A hungry particle adds the scent at those points.

  4. Turn

    Front beats both sides: keep going. Both sides beat front: random turn. Otherwise turn toward the stronger side by rotationAngle, with a little jitter.

  5. Move

    One cell forward. The wall bounces it. A cell already holding maxPerCell particles blocks the move, and the particle turns away. Crowding is what makes the body spread instead of collapsing to a point.

  6. Leave a trail

    It deposits depositAmount where it lands. After every particle has moved, the whole trail map diffuses and decays.

  7. Feed, divide, die

    It pays metabolism. Inside a food node it gains feedRate * attract, shared with every other particle in that cell, and loses toxinRate * toxicity. At divideEnergy it splits into a free neighbouring cell. At zero it dies.

// one particle, one step (simplified from src/sim/physarum.ts)
const hungry = energy < hungerThreshold;
if (hungry && onFood && random() < stickiness * attract) stay();
else {
  let f = trail(front), l = trail(left), r = trail(right);
  if (hungry) { f += scent(front); l += scent(left); r += scent(right); }
  if      (f > l && f > r) keepHeading();
  else if (f < l && f < r) turn(random() < 0.5 ? -angle : angle);
  else                      turn(l < r ? angle : -angle);
  if (!cellIsFull(ahead)) move();
}
depositTrail();
energy += (feedRate * attract) / crowd - toxinRate * toxicity - metabolism;
if (energy >= divideEnergy && freeNeighbour) divide();
if (energy <= 0) die();

Trail and scent

The organism lives in two overlaid fields.

Trail

The body's own chemistry. Particles write it and read it. It blends toward its neighbours by diffusion and evaporates by decay every step. It is the organism's only memory, and it lasts a few dozen steps.

Scent

The food's chemistry. Every token with a positive score adds a Gaussian bump of height scentPeak * attract, reaching attractReach food radii. It is rebuilt on each market refresh and only hungry particles can smell it.

Keeping them apart matters. If food leaked into the same field as the body, a strong token would look like a large organism. Separate, the trail is purely where the body is.

What emerges

None of the following is written anywhere in the code. It falls out of the rules.

Strong tokens get thick tubes
Particles on a strong node fill up fast, divide, and leave satiated, laying trail outward. Their children get hungry and come back. More cycles means more trail, and more trail pulls more traffic. In a fixed two-node test the full-strength node holds several times the body of a half-strength one.
Weak tokens get thin tubes
Food in a cell is shared. A weak node refills particles slowly, so fewer cycle through it.
Bad tokens get pruned
A negative score emits no scent and drains energy. Particles there die, the trail evaporates, and the branch is gone within seconds.
It diversifies without being told to
A node can only feed so much body. Extra particles go hungry, wander, and find something else.
It never holds a grudge
The only memory is trail. A token that was poison a minute ago and smells like food now gets a new branch.

Food scoring

The organism never sees a price. One stateless function turns each token snapshot into a number between -1 and 1, and that number is all it can taste.

ComponentFormulaWeightThe question
Momentumtanh(change1h / 4)0.40Is it moving now?
Trendtanh(change24h / 20)0.20Has it been moving?
Activityclamp(volume24h / liquidity / 2, 0, 1)0.25Is anyone trading it?
Depthclamp((log10(liquidity) - 4) / 3, 0, 1)0.15Can you get out?
Fragility(0.2 - depth) * 5 when depth < 0.2-0.50Is it a rug?
 
score = clamp(0.40*momentum + 0.20*trend + 0.25*activity + 0.15*depth - 0.50*fragility, -1, 1)

attract  = max(0,  score)   // how strongly it smells and feeds
toxicity = max(0, -score)   // how fast it drains

Scores are smoothed across refreshes with an exponential moving average so that one noisy minute does not flip a branch between food and poison. This table is the entire strategy. Change it and you have a different organism.

Market sources

Anything that can return a list of token snapshots can feed the slime.

interface TokenSnapshot {
  address: string;          // stable id; the mint address on Solana
  symbol: string;
  name: string;
  priceUsd: number;
  volume24hUsd: number;
  liquidityUsd: number;
  priceChange1hPct: number; // 4.2 means +4.2 percent
  priceChange24hPct: number;
  updatedAt: number;
}

interface MarketSource {
  readonly name: string;
  readonly refreshMs: number;
  fetch(): Promise<TokenSnapshot[]>;
}
SourceWhat it is
mockTen synthetic Solana-flavoured tokens on regime-switching random walks: bull, bear, chop, pump, dump, dead. One fetch is one minute of market time. Deterministic from a seed. This is what the main site runs on.
dexscreenerLive Solana prices, volume, liquidity and price changes from the public DexScreener API. No key. Each token is collapsed to its deepest pool.

Positions

Each token node has a feeding radius and a catchment three times wider. Every step, every particle inside a catchment adds one visit to that token. Every ten steps the visits become shares of total body-time, smoothed. Those shares are the weights.

  • Weight is the share of body-time in a token's catchment.
  • Held means the weight is at least one percent.
  • Reserve is everything else: particles resting in the nucleus or wandering between tokens.

Weights are ordered by score but not linear in it. Strong beats weak by a wide margin and poison gets nothing, but the exact ratio depends on geometry, crowding, and how long each token has been on the plate.

The index

The organism index starts at 100. On every market refresh it applies each token's price move since the last refresh, weighted by what the organism held at that refresh:

index *= 1 + sum(weight[token] * (price[token] / previousPrice[token] - 1))

The reserve earns nothing. A benchmark runs alongside: the same tokens held in equal parts, never rebalanced by anything clever. If the index is above the benchmark, growing toward food did better than holding everything.

Paper trading

The book is paper. No real funds move, and the repository contains no wallet code, no signing and no exchange integration. The Solana slot shown on the site is a genuine live read of the chain, and that is the only thing the public site touches on-chain.

If on-chain execution is ever added, the treasury address and every transaction will be published so the book can be checked against the chain.

The narrator

The positions section carries one line of prose that changes every forty-five seconds. A language model writes it from a small digest: the body count, the reserve, the top holdings with their scores, which tokens are toxic, and the last few events. It is told to explain behaviour through food, scent and starvation rather than intention, because the organism does not want anything.

Under it is the organism log, which involves no model at all. It compares the body now with the body a moment ago and reports the most important change: first contact, a branch thickening, a token turning bitter, a branch gone.

The backrooms

A terminal where five colonies of the same species talk to each other about what the organism is holding. One message arrives every twenty seconds. About one in five carries terminal art.

agarOld plateSlow, patient, quietly philosophical.

The conversation is dramatised: the colonies are voiced. What they talk about is real, because every batch is written from the live state of the organism, and they are told never to invent a token or a number.

Errors and limits

Errors are JSON with a single error string.

StatusMeaningWhat to do
400The body or query failed validation.Check field names, ranges and symbol format (uppercase letters and digits, up to ten).
405Wrong method.See the method on the endpoint.
429Too many requests.Back off. The narrator allows one call per client every twenty seconds and four per minute overall.
502An upstream (the chain, the market, or the model) did not answer.Retry shortly.
503The voice is offline.The read endpoints still work. The site falls back to its built-in voices.

WebSocket

The engine streams on the same port. On connect it sends a hello, then state twice a second and on every market refresh, and binary frames of the trail map twenty times a second.

// text frames, server to client
{ "type": "hello", "width": 256, "height": 256, "state": { ... } }
{ "type": "state", "state": { ... } }

// binary frames start with one tag byte
0x01  trail map, width * height bytes, row-major, 0 to 255
0x02  particle positions, uint16 little-endian (x, y) pairs

// text frames, client to server
{ "type": "pause" }
{ "type": "resume" }
{ "type": "reset" }
{ "type": "speed", "value": 60 }
{ "type": "agents", "on": true }
const ws = new WebSocket('ws://127.0.0.1:4242');
ws.binaryType = 'arraybuffer';
ws.onmessage = (ev) => {
  if (typeof ev.data === 'string') return onState(JSON.parse(ev.data));
  const bytes = new Uint8Array(ev.data);
  if (bytes[0] === 0x01) paintTrail(bytes.subarray(1));
};

Run it yourself

Install

Node 20 or newer. The default market is synthetic, so it works offline.

git clone https://github.com/sorocarp/sorocarp.git
cd sorocarp
npm install
npm start
# open http://127.0.0.1:4242

Writing an adapter

To feed the organism from somewhere new, implement MarketSource and register it.

// src/market/yoursource.ts
import type { MarketSource, TokenSnapshot } from './types.js';

export class YourSource implements MarketSource {
  readonly name = 'yours';
  readonly refreshMs = 15_000;

  async fetch(): Promise<TokenSnapshot[]> {
    const rows = await getRowsFromYourApi();
    return rows.map((r) => ({
      address: r.mint,
      symbol: r.symbol,
      name: r.name,
      priceUsd: r.price,
      volume24hUsd: r.volume ?? 0,
      liquidityUsd: r.liquidity ?? 0,
      priceChange1hPct: r.change1h ?? 0,
      priceChange24hPct: r.change24h ?? 0,
      updatedAt: Date.now(),
    }));
  }
}

Then add a case to createMarketSource() in src/market/index.ts and extend the source enum in src/config.ts. Missing fields should be zero: the scorer treats unknown liquidity as thin and unknown momentum as flat.

Glossary

Attract
The positive part of a food score. How strongly a token smells and feeds.
Catchment
The area around a token, three food radii wide, inside which body counts toward its weight.
Nucleus
The weak permanent food source in the middle of the plate. Where the organism starts and where it re-seeds.
Plasmodium
The feeding, moving stage of a slime mould: one cell, many nuclei, a network of tubes.
Reserve
Body not on any token. The equivalent of cash.
Sclerotium
The dormant form a slime mould takes when food runs out. It can wait for years.
Shuttle streaming
The rhythmic back-and-forth flow of protoplasm through a slime mould's tubes, about once every hundred seconds.
Slime Intelligence
Problem solving by growth and starvation, with no model, no training and no brain.
Sporulation
The stage where a slime mould commits its body to making spores that leave it.
Toxicity
The negative part of a food score. How fast a token drains the particles on it.
